How it works

Route service, start to finish

You pick the interval. We handle the rest, including the paperwork.

1

We size the trap and set the interval

First visit, we measure the trap or interceptor, check the grease depth and recommend a 30, 60 or 90 day plan you can change any time.

2

Same route day, every cycle

Your kitchen gets a fixed day on our route. We come before service or after close, whichever you prefer, and text when we're on the way.

3

Pump, scrape and inspect

Full pump-out, walls and baffles scraped, lid gasket checked, and the trap refilled with clean water so it works from the first pan.

4

Manifest and log left on site

A disposal manifest for each service and an up-to-date service log in your binder, ready for the health inspector.

Compliance

The paperwork is part of the service

Inspectors want to see when the trap was pumped, how much came out and where it went. We hand you all three.

  • Disposal manifest per service. Volume, date, truck and the licensed facility that received it.
  • Service log on site. Kept current in your binder, with the next scheduled date.
  • Grease depth recorded. So you can show the trap is being pumped before the 25 percent line.
  • Trouble flagged early. Failed baffles, a stuck lid or a trap that's too small for the kitchen get reported before an inspector finds them.
Questions

Grease trap questions

What owners and kitchen managers ask before they put a trap on the route.

How often does my grease trap need to be pumped?

Often enough that grease and solids never reach a quarter of the trap's depth. For a small under-sink trap in a busy kitchen that can be every 30 days. For a 1,000 gallon interceptor at a cafe, 90 days may be fine. We check the level on the first visit and recommend an interval.

Do you service outdoor interceptors as well as indoor traps?

Yes. In-ground interceptors of 500 and 1,000 gallons are the bulk of our route, along with under-sink and floor traps inside the kitchen.

Can you come outside service hours?

Yes. Early morning before prep or after close are both normal route stops. Tell us the window that works and we'll build the route around it.

Do you cover Longview and Kelso?

Yes. The route runs from Vancouver through Battle Ground, Ridgefield and Woodland up to Longview and Kelso.

What happens if I skip a service?

Grease hardens, the trap overflows into the drain line, and the next call is a plumber or the city. Staying on the route is cheaper than one backup.

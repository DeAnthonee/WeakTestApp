Septic care guide

How often to pump, what to watch for, and the simple habits that keep a septic system out of sight and out of mind.

When should I pump my tank?

Estimate your pumping interval from tank size and household size. Most homes need service every 3 to 5 years.

Warning signs of trouble

If you notice any of these, call before it becomes a backup.

  • Slow drains or gurgling sounds in the plumbing
  • Sewage odors inside or near the tank and drain field
  • Soggy ground, standing water or unusually green grass over the drain field
  • Sewage backing up into tubs, showers or floor drains
  • A high-water or pump alarm sounding

Keep your system healthy

Eight habits that add years to a septic system.

Pump on a schedule

Most homes need pumping every 3 to 5 years. Use the calculator above to estimate your interval and keep a record of every service.

Only flush the three P's

Pee, poo and (toilet) paper. Wipes, even "flushable" ones, feminine products, paper towels and dental floss clog pumps and fill tanks fast.

Keep grease out of the drain

Fats, oils and grease harden in the tank and drain field. Wipe pans and put grease in the trash.

Spread out water use

Run one load of laundry a day instead of five on Saturday, fix leaking toilets, and install low-flow fixtures. Less water means a longer-lasting field.

Protect the drain field

Never park or drive on it, keep trees and deep-rooted shrubs away, and direct roof drains and sump pumps elsewhere.

Go easy on the garbage disposal

A disposal adds up to 50% more solids to your tank. Compost or trash food scraps instead, or plan to pump more often.

Skip the additives

Your tank already has the bacteria it needs. Chemical and enzyme additives are unnecessary and some can damage the system.

Know where your system is

Keep a sketch of the tank, lids and drain field location. Risers make lids easy to find and save you digging fees at every service.
